## Start with the question

*Sample article — replace this draft with Kyle’s own writing before publishing.*

A side project often starts with a question that is more interesting than a feature list. Write that question down first. It gives the work a direction without pretending that every screen and interaction has already been decided.

## Make one useful slice

Choose the smallest version that lets you learn something real. A sketch, a working interaction, or a short conversation can reveal more than a long list of planned features.

> Keep the first version small enough that you can change your mind.

As the project takes shape, keep notes about the decisions you made and what you would try next. Those notes can become a better project story than a polished claim written months later.
